# Scenario bank: mundane, dramatic, true (7 Nov 2026)

Goal (owner): events and decisions so true to Thai lives that an elderly Thai mother playing could cry.
Method: four research passes over the owner's files and a verified survey. Everything below is **original wording of situations**; the books are only the evidence that such moments are real. No quotes. Royal scenes are removed (monarchy rule, `ERA-EVENTS-AND-BUDDHISM.md` section 4); only the human core stays.

## The grammar of the cry (design rules)
1. **A small object carries it.** Stolen sweets, a basin, a handkerchief, a list of drug names. Never a speech about love.
2. **The unsaid sentence.** The most painful beat is a person who does not say it (a father's silence at a goodbye, a mother who says "I am fine"). The player's choice is often whether to speak.
3. **Every option is love for someone.** No right answer; each costs a person. Costs are named (money, a year, a relationship), never moralised.
4. **Price the person.** Several scenes turn on a family doing arithmetic on a life (a servant's price, a drug's price, a daughter's price). Show the number, then show who bears it.
5. **Echo.** A choice returns 10 to 40 years later in a different shape (a goodbye at the landing becomes the funeral where the same sweet is on the tray). The recap shows the thread. This is the Mantra 5 "wheel has not arrived yet" made mechanical.
6. **Restraint.** No music cue, no narrator telling the player to feel. Silence and a held object.
7. **Dignity.** Poverty and inequality are shown as constraints on choices, never as spectacle.

## Bank (36 scenes). Format: situation | options and their real costs | the ache | echo
Meters: H health, M money, B bonds, S standing, P peace.

### Childhood
1. **The landing** (R, Four Reigns, 1890s). A household splits; a mother leaves at night with her child. At the landing a friend presses stolen sweets into the child's hand; a boy clings to a post. | Promise to return; keep silent; take the gift and not look back. | Neither child can say anything. | The same sweet reappears at a funeral decades later.
2. **The father's silence** (R). Saying goodbye, a father studies your face and says nothing. | Speak out of turn; crawl away quietly; ask a half-sister's blessing and get a curse. | His silence is the goodbye.
3. **Your name changes how you are called** (R). Children of the principal wife get a courtesy title; you do not. | Accept; ask for a change; pretend not to hear. | Rank arrives as the way your name is said.
4. **Left with grandparents** (S, I). Parents leave for Bangkok for work. | Go with them and lose the grandparents and school; stay and miss the parents for years; split the year and be a visitor in both homes. | Each is love and each costs a home.
5. **The mother who is "away"** (S). | Tell the truth now; wait to be asked; let a relative say it. | Kindness delays and the delay hurts.
6. **The foundling** (R, Many Lives). Before dawn a widow hears a baby at the landing. | Keep him; hand to the headman; ask neighbours. | She nurses him on a dry breast, wanting only that he not cry. | He is the one who bathes her at the end.
7. **Fifty baht** (R). A mother who cannot feed a nine-year-old places her in a richer household. | Sell; keep and starve together; send her to a relative. | The child sits on the porch edge, too afraid to follow. | An old woman asks for the child's name; nobody knows it. *(Structural inequality; handle plainly, no spectacle.)*
8. **Sell ice cream or stay in school** (S, 1970s). A twelve-year-old sees the family cannot feed everyone. | Quit and earn; finish and let siblings go without; part-time and fail both. | Pride and shame in one afternoon.
9. **The birthday gift** (S, I). A richer classmate's party. | Skip; borrow; bring something made by hand. | It shows how others see you.
10. **The dog at the gate** (R, Mom). The man of the house leaves; the dog waits each evening. | n/a for the dog; for the household: sell gold; take in sewing; move to kin. | The dog follows a voice it no longer answers to. | The returning man has nothing; the dog stays.

### Youth
11. **Leaving for the city** (S, I). A teenager is wanted by the family for money. | Go and send money; stay on a failing plot; study and cost the family now. | The bus station.
12. **A mother asks you to leave the robes** (R). The night before ordination she bathed you; later she begs you to come home to farm and a wife. | Disrobe; stay and send money; bring her to live at the temple. | She goes home crying like a child.
13. **The girl who cooled** (R). After a lawsuit over land, the childhood friend you planned to marry greets you like a stranger and weds a richer son. | Confront; let go; wait. | She is polite.
14. **The estate quarrel** (R). A father dies of fever; relatives sue. | Split fast; fight; give it up. | The mother weeps harder over the lawsuit than the funeral.
15. **The official's promise** (I, Khamsing Srinawk, summary only). A clerk offers a road, a well, or a loan if you sign now; nobody can read it. | Sign; refuse and wait; ask a literate neighbour. | Illiteracy as price.
16. **The offered vote** (I). Cash for a vote. | Take and vote as you like; refuse and be marked; take and give it to the temple. | Being bought cheaply.

### Adult life
17. **The motorcar and the horse** (R, 1910s). A husband buys a new machine and rides horses harder. | Enjoy the prestige; press on safety; keep quiet. | Pride and fear arrive together.
18. **The husband who performs youth** (R). A promotion changes how he wants to be addressed; he drinks imported spirits and jokes about a younger wife. | Indulge; object; pretend not to hear. | You see him ageing while he plays young. *(Strip the court setting; keep the marriage.)*
19. **The sister who turns up** (R). After years, thinner, thick-knuckled hands, a paper parcel of remedies. | Press money on her; offer a room; only take the medicine. | She says childlessness is just as well; they are too poor.
20. **Love paid in secret** (R). The sister quietly pays household costs because her husband is too proud to accept. | Hide it; admit it; stop.
21. **Midwife or doctor** (R). In labour, old midwife and fire versus a foreign doctor. | Doctor; midwife; ask the husband. | The pain is worse than before; you fear the pain is telling you it is a girl.
22. **The daughter you prayed for** (R). Third pregnancy, name already chosen, told to no one. | Keep hoping; prepare a boy's name; say nothing.
23. **The letter home** (S, I; Letters from Thailand). A migrant must write to his mother. | Say all is well; tell the truth; send only money. | Silence is also a letter. | She keeps every one, and reads them wrong.
24. **The pills** (R). A shop widow takes harsh pills rather than see a doctor because of shame. | Fetch a doctor against her will; keep her secret; ask her daughter. | She tells the boy kneading her legs to look for himself.
25. **The shunned man** (I; Khamphiphaksa, plot unverified). A rumour with no proof turns neighbours cold. | Leave; fight; stay silent and farm. | The village never apologises.
26. **The long goodbye in the rain** (S, Pira Sudham, unverified). The son who studied does not come back. | Ask him to return; say you are fine; move to his city. | The mother meets him as a stranger.

### Parenthood and the household
27. **The son who gives every coin away** (R). He is home after ten years abroad, idle, generous. | Push him to work; indulge; ask what he wants. | You want him home forever and must plan his future.
28. **He only wants to be your child** (R). | Let it stand; refuse; bargain. | Your love is what keeps him still.
29. **A marriage fails in front of a child** (S; Bright). | Mother takes the child; father stays; grandmother raises him. | The child cannot say what he saw.
30. **The family's second name** (I, flagged). A child is asked to use a different name at school. | Change; keep both; keep the old at home. | The grandmother cannot read the new one.

### Illness, war, prices (era shocks)
31. **The drug list** (R, 1945). The doctor names medicines that cannot be found. | Search every source; accept; sell something. | The son writes the list and runs out of places.
32. **A relative sells at market price** (R). Kinship does not buy a discount. | Buy; refuse; confront.
33. **"My life is not worth that."** (R). The patient does the sum aloud to stop her son spending. | Spend anyway; honour her wish; hunt cheaper. | He sobs while she calculates. *(Candidate to close the game for many players.)*
34. **Refusing to cheat** (R). A shortcut that would pass the cost to strangers. | Cheat; refuse; take half. | She stays honest because someone else would pay.
35. **Night alarm** (R, Mom). Planes overhead, the household digs in each night. | Obey and lose sleep; sleep on; send the child away. | The one night you do not move is the night it matters.
36. **The long visit** (I, composite). An elderly parent in the village; the child sends money, not time. | Ask them to come; say you are fine; move to the city. | "Do not worry."
