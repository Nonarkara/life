# Lessons from nonharvard.wordpress.com — dilemmas for Life2.0

Research date: 26 October 2026. Source: the author's blog *100 Days of Writing* (nonharvard.wordpress.com, 126 entries read through the public WordPress API, 2015–2025), cross-checked against the author's own digests in `~/Documents/SecondBrain/Soul/` (Blog-Archive-Index, Minimalism-Time, Love-Relationships, Character-Profile).

## How to read this file

- **Provenance tags.**
  - `STATED-BLOG`: the lesson and its reasoning appear in the named post(s). The text here is a paraphrase.
  - `STATED-BRIEF`: the author named the lesson directly when he commissioned this research. The blog has related reasoning but does not state the lesson in these words.
  - `INFERENCE`: my extrapolation from his stated views. It is a game-design proposal, not his position.
- **Effects** use the game's resource names: money, time, health, mind (reasoning and emotional skills), relationships, contentment/Ikigai. They are directional design intents only, not calibrated values. Per `foundation.md`, nothing here is a probability estimate, and nothing should feed a single moral score.
- **STOP** means the simulation should pause time and require an explicit choice. It is reserved for irreversible or life-shaping decisions.
- **Reveal** is what the player should be able to see afterwards. It must be traceable to a concrete record, such as a ledger line, a missed event or a history annotation, never a moral verdict.
- **Privacy.** The blog names friends, a colleague and a partner. All of them are made generic here. The blog's account of his worst period includes interpersonal conflict, and that is reduced to a generic collapse with no allegations.
- **Quotes.** At most one short attributed quote per lesson. Everything else is paraphrase.

## 1. The deferred retirement

**Lesson.** A life planned around "after I retire" can end before retirement arrives, so money saved for later is worth little if health and time are gone. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 21: On Duty — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2015/10/06/day-21-on-duty/ ; Day 39: On Time — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2015/11/25/day-39-on-time/ ; Day 26: On Death — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2015/10/14/day-26-on-death/

His reasoning: his father served one job for about four decades. He expected a pension, then travel, a dog and drawing. He died about two years after retiring. The family was not prepared.

**Dilemma.** Age 30–45. *Your employer offers a secure post with a pension at 60. It means long hours and no sabbatical.*
- **A. Take it and defer everything.** Immediate: money up, time down. Delayed (15–30 yrs): a large pension balance, but a "planned for retirement" list that keeps growing. If health fails early, the list is never used.
- **B. Take it, but spend a fixed share of time now on one deferred wish** (travel, art). Immediate: slightly less money saved, some leisure time. Delayed: a smaller pension, the wish partly lived, contentment steadier.
- **C. Decline it for flexible, less secure work.** Immediate: money volatile, time up. Delayed: thinner retirement savings and more lived experience. Exposed to income shocks (see lesson 2).

**STOP.** Yes.

**Reveal.** A "Deferred until retirement" ledger that lists each postponed wish next to its age at deferral. At death it shows which were never redeemed. Show it beside the pension balance and the age at death.

## 2. Did you save enough for retirement?

**Lesson.** Saving too little leaves old age exposed to poverty and to depending on others. Saving everything for later throws away the present. The test is a runway, not a maximum. `STATED-BRIEF`. The blog's own emphasis is the opposite failure (lessons 1 and 3). The balance between the two is `INFERENCE`.

**Sources.** Day 21 and Day 39 (above). Day 63: I Wish I Had Never Done a PhD — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2016/05/15/day-64-i-wish-i-had-never-done-a-phd/ (side work during the PhD to send money home, and a household that was never financially secure).

**Dilemma.** Age 25–35, then yearly. *Each raise asks the same question: where does the extra go?*
- **A. Spend it on lifestyle.** Immediate: contentment up briefly. Delayed (20+ yrs): no runway at 60. Work is forced to continue into old age, or support falls to relatives, which costs their money and their time.
- **B. Save a steady share automatically.** Immediate: small. Delayed: a runway measured in years. Old-age choices stay open.
- **C. Save almost all of it and live austerely.** Immediate: relationships and experiences thin. Delayed: a large balance and the same deferred-life risk as 1A.

**STOP.** No. This is an annual setting. A one-off STOP should fire at the first raise to explain the setting.

**Reveal.** Retirement runway in months at age 60, projected and then actual. Also an "old-age dependence" line: who paid, and how much time it cost them.

## 3. Saving money for someone instead of spending time with them

**Lesson.** Earning money so that you can one day treat someone you love risks that person dying before "one day." The time together was the thing you were saving for. `STATED-BLOG`

**Source.** Day 36: Worst Mistakes People Make in Their 20s — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2015/11/08/day-35-worst-mistakes-people-make-in-their-20s/ (item 2). He worked and saved through his twenties to take his father on a trip. By the time he could afford it, his father had died.

**Dilemma.** Age 22–35, while a parent is ageing. *A parent loves travel. You could take extra work now and pay for a big trip in five years, or visit often now and cheaply.*
- **A. Extra work now, big trip later.** Immediate: money up, time with the parent down. Delayed (3–10 yrs): the trip happens only if the parent is still alive and well.
- **B. Frequent small visits now.** Immediate: money down a little, time with the parent up. Delayed: shared memories accumulate, with no dependence on the parent's survival.
- **C. Both, with less rest.** Immediate: health and mind strained. Delayed: burnout risk.

**STOP.** Yes, when a parent's health event first appears.

**Reveal.** "Hours with [parent]" by year, shown on the parent's health history. The day the parent dies, show any unspent "for them" savings beside the hours actually spent.

## 4. The funeral you miss abroad

**Lesson.** Pursuing a goal far from home means accepting that you may miss the deaths of people you love. A family can release you from that duty, but the cost is still real. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 39: On Time (above). Day 63 (above). He lost grandparents and his father while studying abroad, and he grieved alone each time.

**Dilemma.** Age 22–40, while studying or working abroad. *A grandparent dies. Flying home costs a month's savings and an exam or a deadline. Your family tells you to stay and keep going.*
- **A. Stay, as they asked.** Immediate: the goal is protected, mind down (grief alone). Delayed: the goal is reached, plus a permanent "absent" mark in the family record.
- **B. Go home.** Immediate: money down, goal delayed. Delayed: family bond up, grief processed. The opportunity may or may not come back.
- **C. Stay, and set aside a ritual and a phone call.** Immediate: partial mitigation.

**STOP.** Yes.

**Reveal.** A family record of presence and absence at the deaths of people you were close to, each with what the trip would have cost and the goal it protected.

## 5. The "carpet": saying yes to every request

**Lesson.** Being generous with every request makes you easy to use. People keep stepping on a carpet that never says no, and the time and money you give away come out of your own Ikigai. `STATED-BRIEF`. Related blog reasoning: you cannot please everyone (Day 36, items 6–7). Stay away from selfish people (Day 75). Saying "I don't have time" is a moral judgement, not a scheduling excuse (Day 39).

**Sources.** Day 36 (above). Day 75: 12 Rules for Life — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2018/07/25/day-75-12-rules-for-life/ ; Day 39 (above).

**Dilemma.** Recurring, age 18–60. *A friend, relative or colleague asks for a loan, an unpaid favour, or to cover their shift, and it is the fourth time this year.*
- **A. Yes, again.** Immediate: their relationship score up, your money or time down. Delayed (5–15 yrs): requests rise in frequency, unrepaid loans pile up, and your own projects stall. The relationship does not deepen.
- **B. Yes, with a condition** (repayment date, reciprocity). Immediate: slight friction. Delayed: a sorting effect, where reciprocal relationships deepen and extractive ones fade.
- **C. No, kindly.** Immediate: relationship down with the requester. Delayed: time protected, and genuinely close relationships unaffected.
- **D. Give freely, but only to chosen causes.** This is his stated view that freedom includes serving others (Day 36). Immediate: money or time out. Delayed: contentment/Ikigai up, without the drift of request volume.

**STOP.** Only the first time a requester crosses a threshold (third unrepaid request). Afterwards it becomes a standing policy the player can change.

**Reveal.** A "Given away" ledger split into *chosen* and *asked-for*. Show cumulative hours and money per requester, repaid or not. Then draw a line from that total to a stalled personal goal, for example "these 900 hours ≈ the unfinished book."

## 6. Married and parents too soon

**Lesson.** Marrying and having children early, because relatives and convention expect it, can consume the whole time and money budget. That leaves no room for any other source of Ikigai. `STATED-BRIEF`. In the blog he refuses to marry before he understands why marriage exists (Day 40). He describes a peer whose script was simply degree, marriage, child (Day 63). He separates "a reason to live," such as paying a family's bills, from "a life worth living" (Day 33).

**Sources.** Day 40: On Sex, Part 1 — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2015/11/27/day-40-on-sex-part-1/ ; Day 33: What Makes Life Worth Living — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2015/10/29/day-32-what-makes-life-worth-living/ ; Day 63 (above).

**Dilemma.** Age 22–30. *Relatives press: you are getting old, just pick someone. A partner is willing. Your savings and career are still near zero.*
- **A. Marry and have a child now.** Immediate: family approval up, time and money committed for about 20 years. Delayed (5–20 yrs): the slots for study abroad, a career pivot and art are closed or delayed. For some players family becomes the Ikigai, and that is a valid outcome.
- **B. Marry, but delay children.** Immediate: partial approval. Delayed: options stay open longer, with fertility and age trade-offs later.
- **C. Wait until you know why you want it.** Immediate: social pressure and loneliness risk. Delayed: options kept open, with the risk of never finding the partner.
- **D. Never marry.** Immediate: pressure continues. Delayed: freedom and solitude, with care in old age to be arranged separately.

**STOP.** Yes.

**Reveal.** A stacked time budget from 22 to 45, showing care, work and everything else, with the "everything else" band named per player goal. Mark the age at which each goal became infeasible. Family must not be shown as a loss: show what was gained and what was closed, side by side.

## 7. Freedom as doing whatever you want

**Lesson.** Treating freedom as doing whatever you want in your twenties hurts other people and yourself. Freedom has to be freedom *from* something, such as your own worst impulses. `STATED-BLOG`

**Source.** Day 36 (above), introduction. He calls his twenties a decade of regretted behaviour done in the name of freedom.

**Dilemma.** Age 18–28. *A shortcut would get you something you want by misleading someone who trusts you.*
- **A. Take it.** Immediate: gain. Delayed (5–15 yrs): reputation damage that may surface, guilt (mind down), and relationships that cannot be repaired.
- **B. Refuse it.** Immediate: no gain. Delayed: trust compounds.
- **C. Ask an older mentor first.** Immediate: time cost. Delayed: better judgement, with a mind (reasoning) gain.

**STOP.** No. These are small, frequent events, and the lesson is their accumulation.

**Reveal.** A list of the people affected by your shortcuts, with what happened to the relationship later. Some outcomes stay unknown, as the blog notes for his own case.

## 8. Borrowing to skip the queue

**Lesson.** Impatience paid for with debt can multiply when a crisis hits. In his case borrowed money for study abroad tripled after the 2008 downturn and took about five years to repay. `STATED-BLOG`

**Source.** Day 36 (item 5).

**Dilemma.** Age 22–30. *Others spend years saving before they go abroad. A bank will lend you the full amount now.*
- **A. Borrow now.** Immediate: the opportunity starts early. Delayed (1–7 yrs): if a macro shock hits, the debt balloons and years of income go to repayment.
- **B. Save first.** Immediate: delay. Delayed: lower risk, with a chance the window closes.
- **C. Apply for grants repeatedly** (lesson 9). Immediate: rejections and time. Delayed: debt-free if it succeeds.

**STOP.** Yes.

**Reveal.** A debt history line with the shock annotated, and "years of income spent repaying." It should replay under the same shock with the saving branch for comparison.

## 9. The scholarship with a chain

**Lesson.** Free money with a long service bond can trap you. Some bonded scholars come home to jobs far below their training. Persistent, unbonded grant applications, even through dozens of rejections, can keep you free. `STATED-BLOG`

**Source.** Day 121: Free Rides to Harvard, MIT, Oxford — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2025/04/17/day-121-how-i-never-paid-a-penny-for-my-education/ . He describes about 20 rejections before one fellowship and about 50 before the next, with no debt in the end.

**Dilemma.** Age 21–30. *A government scholarship covers everything, but you must serve at home for twice the study period or repay several times the cost.*
- **A. Accept the bond.** Immediate: certainty. Delayed (5–15 yrs): a guaranteed post that may waste the specialisation. Leaving early has a large repayment.
- **B. Keep applying for unbonded grants.** Immediate: repeated rejections (mind down, then resilience up). Delayed: freedom of placement if it succeeds; if it fails, a delayed or lost opportunity.
- **C. Self-fund with a loan.** See lesson 8.

**STOP.** Yes.

**Reveal.** A rejection counter that turns into an acceptance, or does not. For the bond branch, a "skills used" versus "skills trained" comparison at the post you were assigned.

## 10. The doctorate's opportunity cost

**Lesson.** A long academic path costs your youth, and youth cannot be bought back. It may sharpen thinking, but by the time it ends others may have built money and influence first. `STATED-BLOG`. He calls this a regret and also credits the PhD with his critical thinking.

**Source.** Day 63 (above). Also Day 64: My Honest Résumé — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2016/05/16/day-64-my-resume/

**Dilemma.** Age 24–32. *You have a doctoral offer: six or more years on a stipend, side jobs, and family needs at home.*
- **A. Do the doctorate.** Immediate: low income, long hours. Delayed (6–15 yrs): mind (reasoning) way up, money behind peers, and a hard academic job market.
- **B. Go to industry.** Immediate: money up. Delayed: earlier wealth, with less depth.
- **C. A shorter master's, then decide.** Immediate: a middle path.

**STOP.** Yes.

**Reveal.** Two lines against age: cumulative earnings versus a "reasoning skill" history. Also a list of family events that happened during the programme.

## 11. Your health, your father's lungs

**Lesson.** Neglecting health when you are young collects its cost decades later and turns retirement into years of illness. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 36 (item 1). His father quit smoking at about 40, too late, and lived with failing lungs for two decades. Also Day 124: The Only Thing That Matters — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2025/04/21/124-the-only-thing-that-matters/

**Dilemma.** Age 16–40, recurring. *Friends smoke, drink and eat out nightly. Joining is how you belong.*
- **A. Join fully.** Immediate: relationships up. Delayed (15–30 yrs): chronic illness, weekly clinic visits, and care costs that land on family.
- **B. Join socially but abstain.** Immediate: slight friction. Delayed: health preserved.
- **C. Quit later, at 40.** Delayed: partial recovery, with permanent damage.

**STOP.** No (habits). A one-off STOP when the first diagnosis appears.

**Reveal.** A health history with the habit's start and stop annotated, and a "years in poor health" count. A life review that compares the same life with the habit removed, under the same shocks.

## 12. When health fails, everything else stops

**Lesson.** Health is the vehicle for every other goal. A sudden injury can cancel a full calendar overnight, so the people who cope keep slack and backup plans. `STATED-BLOG`

**Source.** Day 124 (above). A minor bite became days of hospital pain and cancelled work.

**Dilemma.** Any age. *A small injury hurts more than it should. You have important meetings this week.*
- **A. Push through.** Immediate: meetings kept. Delayed (days to weeks): the condition worsens, a longer hospital stay, more lost time.
- **B. Get treated now and cancel.** Immediate: work and reputation cost. Delayed: faster recovery.

**STOP.** Yes, for the acute event.

**Reveal.** A "calendar lost to illness" strip. A note on whether the player kept slack (savings runway, flexible work) that absorbed the shock.

## 13. One employer, one basket

**Lesson.** People who spend years at one company often have no idea how else to earn a living when they are laid off. Small, cheap experiments and open options are protection against a black swan. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 79: On Design Thinking Part 3: The Black Swan — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2018/08/01/day-79-on-design-thinking-part-3-the-black-swan/ ; Day 87: The Anti-Fragile Manifesto — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2018/08/14/day-86-the-anti-fragile-manifesto/

**Dilemma.** Age 28–50. *Your job is stable. A side skill or project would cost five hours a week.*
- **A. Stay focused on the job only.** Immediate: rest preserved. Delayed (5–15 yrs): if the firm closes, months of unemployment.
- **B. Run a small side experiment.** Immediate: time and rest down. Delayed: a second income path, which may also become an Ikigai.

**STOP.** No. When the layoff shock fires, STOP and offer only the options the player's history made possible.

**Reveal.** At the layoff, show "feasible paths" next to the past choices that created or failed to create each one.

## 14. Tolerating a bad boss

**Lesson.** Work that gives life meaning needs a boss you respect. Staying under a bad one invites being made the scapegoat. `STATED-BLOG`. He attributes the rule partly to his father.

**Sources.** Day 75 (above). Day 117: Notes from My Personal Experience Working for a Top Design Firm — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2021/05/27/day-117-notes-from-my-personal-experience-working-for-a-top-design-firm/ ; Day 114: Minimalist Benefits of Working from Home — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2021/03/21/day-114-minimalist-benefits-of-working-from-home/ (office drama, not commuting, was his main source of fatigue).

**Dilemma.** Age 22–55. *Your manager takes credit for your work and blames you for failures. Leaving means no salary for a few months.*
- **A. Endure.** Immediate: income kept. Delayed (2–10 yrs): mind and health down, and a blame event becomes more likely.
- **B. Leave once you have a runway.** Immediate: money down. Delayed: recovery; it depends on lesson 2's savings.
- **C. Confront it formally.** Immediate: risk. Delayed: the outcome is uncertain.

**STOP.** Yes, when the first scapegoat event happens.

**Reveal.** Mind history annotated with workplace events. Whether the savings runway made leaving feasible.

## 15. Letting others define you

**Lesson.** Letting other people decide who you love, what you study and what you do with your body is a slow death of meaning. Live your own path at your own pace. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 36 (item 4). Day 75 (rule: live your own life). Day 98: What I Know for Sure — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2018/10/06/day-98-what-i-know-for-sure/

**Dilemma.** Age 17–25. *Parents want you in a respected field. You want something else.*
- **A. Follow their choice.** Immediate: family approval up, financial safety. Delayed (10–20 yrs): a contentment/Ikigai gap that grows and a mid-life pivot pressure.
- **B. Follow yours.** Immediate: conflict and less support. Delayed: uncertain money, higher Ikigai if it works.
- **C. Do both: their degree plus your side craft.** Immediate: time strain.

**STOP.** Yes.

**Reveal.** A "whose goal was this?" tag on every major milestone, with the player's own stated priorities beside it.

## 16. Friendships that don't last forever

**Lesson.** Not every friendship can last. Keeping people who drain you, or whose values conflict with yours, costs your scarcest resource. Keep the good memories and let them go without malice. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 36 (items 6–7). Day 39 (above). Day 77: Gossiping — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2018/07/27/day-77-gossiping-or-by-definition-a-crime-against-humanity/

**Dilemma.** Age 20–60. *A long-time friend's circle runs on gossip and drinking. You leave every meeting feeling worse.*
- **A. Keep going out of loyalty.** Immediate: belonging. Delayed: time and mind drain.
- **B. Drift away kindly.** Immediate: a small loss of belonging. Delayed: time reclaimed, and room for new relationships.
- **C. Try to change the friendship.** Immediate: friction, with an uncertain outcome.

**STOP.** No.

**Reveal.** Time spent per relationship against the mind and contentment change after each contact.

## 17. Comparing yourself to others

**Lesson.** Comparing your situation with other people's is the fastest route to feeling bad. Most of what you see online is not who people really are. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 98 (above). Day 99: Why Reading News Isn't Good for You — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2018/12/14/day-99-why-i-dont-read-news/

**Dilemma.** Age 15–50. *A peer posts a new car, a wedding, a promotion. A loan would let you match them.*
- **A. Match them.** Immediate: brief contentment. Delayed: debt and a treadmill of comparison.
- **B. Mute it and return to your own path.** Immediate: none. Delayed: mind steadier.

**STOP.** No.

**Reveal.** A list of comparison-driven purchases with their total cost and the months of runway they consumed.

## 18. Less, but better

**Lesson.** Owning less, travelling light and buying fewer but better things gives you more freedom and contentment, and it lowers the money you need to stay free. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 68: A Minimalist (or -ism) Manifesto — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2016/07/05/day-68-a-minimalism-manifesto/ ; Day 46: 16 Things I Never Have — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2015/12/17/day-46-what-i-never-have/ ; Day 110: 2 Years of Traveling the World with Just 1 Backpack — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2020/06/10/day-110-2-years-of-traveling-the-world-with-just-1-backpack-an-accidental-minimalist-tale/

**Dilemma.** Age 25–45. *Income rises. Do your monthly costs rise with it?*
- **A. Upgrade everything.** Immediate: comfort. Delayed: fixed costs locked in, and a smaller runway that makes leaving bad work impossible (links to 14).
- **B. Hold costs flat.** Immediate: none. Delayed: runway grows, and freedom of choice with it.

**STOP.** No. An annual setting.

**Reveal.** "Months you could live without income," shown against the fixed monthly cost.

## 19. Rock bottom: staying, or going light

**Lesson.** At his lowest point, he left the place that kept reminding him of the loss. Travelling with almost nothing, together with books and kind strangers, helped him back to life. Self-worth does not have to depend on old friends who left. `STATED-BLOG`. The details are deliberately generic here.

**Sources.** Day 108: How I Got Out of the Darkest Time — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2020/06/02/day-108-how-i-got-out-of-the-darkest-time/ ; Day 111: How 'Light and Cheap' Travel Helped Me Escape Death — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2020/06/18/day-111-how-light-and-cheap-travel-helped-me-escape-death/ ; Day 36 (item 8: moving can change how you see the problem).

**Dilemma.** Any adult age, after a collapse (job lost, social circle gone). *Do you stay where everything reminds you, or leave with one backpack?*
- **A. Stay and wait.** Immediate: no cost. Delayed: health and mind keep sliding.
- **B. Go light and cheap for one more year.** Immediate: money minimal, risk. Delayed: mind recovers, and new relationships form.
- **C. Seek treatment and support where you are.** Immediate: cost and effort. Delayed: recovery.

**STOP.** Yes. Design note: depict suicidal ideation carefully and always offer help resources. Recovery must remain possible on every branch.

**Reveal.** A mind history with the collapse and the turning point annotated, and the new relationships formed afterwards.

## 20. Planning years in advance

**Lesson.** Plans almost never run on schedule. He finished a 100-day project about three years late, and he treats that as the nature of life rather than a failure. Plan loosely, with slack. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 98 (above). Day 36 (item 12, which points to Day 26: On Death).

**Dilemma.** Age 20–40. *You commit your whole savings to a ten-year plan with no buffer.*
- **A. All in.** Delayed: one shock breaks the plan.
- **B. Plan with a buffer.** Delayed: the plan bends but survives.

**STOP.** No.

**Reveal.** Planned timeline versus actual, with each delay attributed to choice, circumstance or chance (per `foundation.md`).

## 21. Afraid to fail in public

**Lesson.** Confidence is not a precondition for action. It is built by failing repeatedly in public and continuing. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 123: Confidence Is Built by Failing. Full Stop. — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2025/04/18/day-123-confidence-is-built-by-failing-full-stop/ ; Day 121 (rejections).

**Dilemma.** Age 15–35. *You are invited to speak, apply or perform, and you are likely to do badly.*
- **A. Decline.** Immediate: safety. Delayed: skill and confidence stagnate, and the opportunities stop coming.
- **B. Accept and fail.** Immediate: mind down briefly. Delayed: a skill curve that rises with each attempt.

**STOP.** No.

**Reveal.** An attempts-versus-outcomes chart showing that confidence followed attempts, not the other way round.

## 22. Kind, or right?

**Lesson.** Fighting to be right has cost friendships over things you were not even sure of. Kindness usually serves you better than winning the argument. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 75 (rule: be kind, not right). Day 108 (choosing kindness over correctness).

**Dilemma.** Any age. *A friend is wrong in public. Correcting them would humiliate them.*
- **A. Correct them.** Immediate: you are right. Delayed: the relationship cools.
- **B. Let it go, or tell them privately.** Delayed: the relationship holds.

**STOP.** No.

**Reveal.** Relationship history annotated with conflicts that were won.

## 23. Luck needs a prepared player

**Lesson.** Success is roughly half luck, but only the determined recognise luck when it arrives. Without preparation you simply blame others. `STATED-BLOG`

**Sources.** Day 75 (on determination and luck). Day 28: I Was Just Lucky — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2015/10/19/day-28-who-ive-become-an-outli/

**Dilemma.** Age 18–40. *A rare opportunity appears, but it requires a skill you would have needed to build earlier.*
- The options depend on prior investment. If the skill is present, a door opens. If not, it is shown as "seen but unusable."

**STOP.** Yes, when a chance event appears.

**Reveal.** A "missed openings" list: each lucky event and the preparation it required.

## 24. The work-for-later ethic of the grandparents

**Lesson.** His grandparents lived by rising early, eating fast and working hard. They found the pleasure in seeing other people happy. Hard work can be an Ikigai when it serves others, and drudgery when it serves nothing. `STATED-BLOG` (the story). Its use as a dilemma is `INFERENCE`.

**Source.** Day 39 (above).

**Dilemma.** Age 30–60, running a small family business. *Expand and work every hour, or keep it small and serve regulars well?*
- **A. Expand.** Immediate: money up, time down. Delayed: wealth, health strain.
- **B. Stay small and excellent** (Kodawari, Day 116). Delayed: moderate money, high contentment.

**STOP.** No.

**Reveal.** Contentment/Ikigai history annotated with "whom this work served."

## 25. The quick life review: what would your father say

**Lesson.** Regret at the end of a life collects around time not spent and wishes deferred, rather than around money. `INFERENCE`, synthesised from lessons 1, 3 and 11. Use it as an end-of-life reflection, not a scored dilemma.

**Sources.** Day 26: On Death; Day 27: Cicero, On Aging — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2015/10/16/day-27-on-aging/ ; Day 17: How Do I Want to Get Old — https://nonharvard.wordpress.com/2015/10/02/day-16-how-do-i-want-to-get-old/

**Dilemma.** At death, or as an optional pause at 60. No choices; a reflection screen.

**STOP.** Yes (end screen).

**Reveal.** Three lists side by side: deferred wishes never redeemed (1), hours given away unchosen (5), and hours spent with people who died (3). Each item links back to the decision that created it.

## Notes for the designer

- **Tension to keep, not resolve.** Lesson 5 (stop being a carpet) and his stated ideal that the fullest freedom is to serve others (Day 36) pull against each other. The game should separate *chosen* giving from *extracted* giving rather than punish generosity.
- **His retirement lesson is mostly the opposite failure.** The blog warns against deferring life for a pension more than against undersaving. Lesson 2 makes the undersaving side playable, so present 1 and 2 as one trade-off.
- **Lesson 6 is not anti-family.** For some players, family is their Ikigai. The design should show what an early commitment closed and what it gave, without a verdict.
- **What could be verified.** The blog content was read through the public WordPress API on 26 October 2026, 126 entries. Nothing here is a probability estimate.
