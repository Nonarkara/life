# Fragility and care ·30October2026

Design: a good plan does not make a person invulnerable. Named emergencies are
external events; effort never grants immunity. EQ may buffer the authored mood
response, never prevent infection or restore breathing. Immediate care is already
received in these narratives. The later plain-language question concerns daily
support, expense and recovery; it is not an emergency-treatment quiz.

Added asthma/recurrent asthma, dengue/recurrent dengue, nonfatal river accident
and a parent's tuberculosis treatment. Recurrence requires a recorded first
illness. The asthma seed subgroup is an authored narrative distribution, NOT a
genetic test, prevalence estimate or diagnosis. The named disease costs, index
changes, event frequency and timing are all game tuning; they are not medical
predictions or an account of any identifiable person's medical history.

Primary references, checked30October2026:
- [WHO asthma](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/asthma): chronic
  condition with environmental and familial risk factors; treatment can control
  symptoms. Do not say a person caused an attack by failing to be disciplined.
- [WHO dengue](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/dengue-and-severe-dengue):
  repeat infection is possible; a previous infection is not universal immunity.
- [WHO tuberculosis](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/tuberculosis):
  infectious and treatable; no inherited-TB mechanic.
- [WHO drowning](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/drowning):
  nonfatal outcomes occur; living near water is not a moral failing.

Limits: current yearly-event engine starts after the age10 station. It does not
model infant emergencies, maternal mortality, clinical severity or individualized
survival. Those require careful age/family chronology and care-access mechanics;
this release does not pretend they exist. No generic fever is silently diagnosed
as dengue. Public text uses original fictional situations, not private family
biographies. No graphic injury imagery is added.
