# Decision rhythm — 29 October 2026

## What was observed

Played the official [1 in 8 Billion](https://1in8b.life/en/) browser build 0.8.80
from an existing Botswana life at 15. “Next decision” led to an age-16 education
dilemma. The life held at that age while the alternatives remained open. Choosing
an equivalency route changed the education record, job-seeking state and available
focus actions. This is a narrow direct play observation, not an exhaustive audit
of every country, death, ending or feature. No source code, artwork or prose copied.

Its useful rhythm is ordinary life → personally situated interruption → specific
choice → changed opportunities → a record worth comparing with another life.
Random birth makes the constraints personal. Small actions make an eventual
fork matter. Simplicity helps the player read consequences without running a
spreadsheet. These are hypotheses about engagement, not proof of virality:
we have no acquisition, retention or player-interview data from the original.

[Sid Meier’s GDC talk on interesting decisions](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1015756/Do-Say-The-Right-Thing)
is a design reference for consequential choices rather than obvious best answers.
[Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski (2006)](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf)
connect autonomy, competence and relatedness with enjoyment and future play.
Those findings guide questions to test; they do not certify this implementation.

## Applied to Life2.0

The existing thirty station questions already contain trade-offs. Their worksheet
presentation buried those stories. Now one complete dilemma owns the reading
surface; its authored context, options and costs stay together. Answering shows
the recorded event, actual resource changes, uncertainty result where applicable,
and effort committed by that answer. The player explicitly continues to the next
choice or five-year planning. No automatic/default answer or countdown pressure.

All unresolved incidents, station questions and unacknowledged outcomes stop the
actors as well as the age clock, regardless of selected transport speed. Planning
runs the scene gently; fast travel eases into the final year before the next stop.
The reading surface can be set aside to inspect the existing world and instruments.
Saved answers persist; double charging and wrong-age answers are rejected.
Counterfactuals enumerate options affordable in the restored decision state, and
never publish an alternative whose application was rejected.

A childhood job can compete with study without implying the child quit school.
The recorded work/study scene persists through its actual travel period. Existing
art, palette ownership, seed, languages, thirty questions, encounters, finances,
world choices and saves remain. No compulsory art-model service or new engine.

## Outcomes and limits

Three scripted strategies in one fixed game world produced distinct wealth,
skills, wellbeing and life lengths (see review/decision-play/branch-sample.json).
This illustrates branch diversity, not population evidence, a fair ranking of
careers, or an optimal strategy. Extreme success is not guaranteed or necessary:
a relationship, financial security and health can conflict in an interesting life.
No balance coefficients were changed to manufacture dramatic results.

Next human test: can a new player describe what each option sacrifices, identify
what actually changed, explain an effort commitment, and deliberately play a
different path? Observe whether they voluntarily replay and why. Fun and retention
remain unverified until that test; neither screenshot approval nor test count
answers it. This pass does not resolve inherited foreign-city label cropping,
phone Plan scrolling or missing grandmother artwork.
