## 3. Buddhism as a system, not a backdrop

From `rajadhon` (firm): Theravada of the Ceylonese school adopted at Sukhothai and kept to this day; earlier Dvaravati Buddhism and Srivijaya Mahayana; Hindu and animist layers mixed in; the temple as the stage for festival drama; temple mural painting; sculpture mostly Buddha images; religion shaping literature; farming that left time for contemplation.

Everything below is `needs-source` and becomes data only once cited:
- **Life-cycle points:** ordination as a monk for a time (a young man's merit and a family's), novice ordination, merit-making, funeral rites and funeral books (the form in the supplied book), Buddhist Lent, Songkran, temple school as the first education, the abbot as adviser.
- **Mechanic:** merit is a **household value, not a score**. It changes choices (who you can lean on, how a death is held) and never "wins". No karma points, no ranking of faiths. Other faiths in the country (Muslim south, Christian communities, Chinese temples) are present as people in the scene, engaged inside their own logic (Mantra 6).
- **Tone:** quiet, never preached; the game does not tell the player what to believe.
